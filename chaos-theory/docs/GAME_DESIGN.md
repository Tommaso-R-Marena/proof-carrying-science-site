# Game design

Player promise: move immediately, see a clear goal, try a trick, finish or retry within 75 seconds. The game is about playful disruption; participation in research is optional.

The featured Mailroom episode requires three parcels stolen and carried to the hideout. It supports a notice-and-follow route and a foam/intercept route, plus a hacked central shortcut and decoy distraction. Parcel burden and successive security escalations make each return trip matter. Vault supports fake clearance or a hacked door, then a return trip with the relic. Reactor is a route-planning race against a device-repair technician. Courier, guard, and technician have distinct silhouettes/colors and visible state labels. A decoy competes with guard pursuit; walls break sight; conveyors can be reversed; device repair can undo a shortcut.

Foam recharges in 0.7 seconds, decoy in 5, hacker in 2, and beacon in 3. Deliberate non-foam gadget uses need a released trigger between presses. Gadgets have no consumable purchase economy. Dash has a 75-tick cooldown and short invulnerability. A guard hit costs a heart and 100 points, with a recovery window. Mailroom guards have a 120-degree wall-occluded sight cone, a visible 65-unit close-detection ring, and a 30-tick suspicion interval. Hauls and the 30/50-second marks raise alert; the final haul calls backup with a three-second arrival warning.

Score consists of one-time objective awards, remaining-time bonus, first-use gadget variety, capped first-time device/robot tricks, and hit penalties. Repeating an already-scored action cannot farm points. Bronze marks a completed score, silver starts at 1,500 and gold at 1,900. Mailroom objective awards total 1,200 after three completed hauls, with 4 points per remaining second, a 150-point clean-run bonus and a capped 120-point bonus for actually using both stealing methods. Earlier easy mailroom scores are archived outside the new ranking. First win unlocks pink; 4,000 combined best points unlock gold. Cosmetic settings alter the scarf only. Local scores are editable browser data, not authenticated rankings.

The first mission updates the HUD at every pickup and bank; three visible circles and a security indicator summarize progress. The courier returns physically to intake and brakes to reload. Camera hit-stop/shake, confetti, expressive original robot art and distinct synthesized sounds supply game feedback; motion/mute options remain. Pulsing markers, goal labels, gadget-range outlines, and short inline hints help without a tutorial wall. Sound is optional, synthesized only after a gesture, with mute in Options and Pause. Reduced-motion mode removes decorative movement; essential object movement remains.

Known scope: no co-op, editor, remote leaderboard, adaptive learned robot, background music, or claim of human-tested fun. Browser playtesting demonstrates usable controls and mission completion; a real novice playtest is the next quality gate.
