# Game design

Player promise: move immediately, see a clear goal, try a trick, finish or retry within 75 seconds. The game is about playful disruption; participation in research is optional.

Mailroom supports a sign-and-follow route and a foam/intercept route. Vault supports fake clearance or a hacked door, then a return trip with the relic. Reactor is a route-planning race against a device-repair technician. Courier, guard, and technician have distinct silhouettes/colors and visible state labels. A decoy competes with guard pursuit; walls break sight; conveyors can be reversed; device repair can undo a shortcut.

Foam recharges in 0.7 seconds, decoy in 5, hacker in 2, and beacon in 3. Deliberate non-foam gadget uses need a released trigger between presses. Gadgets have no consumable purchase economy. Dash has a 75-tick cooldown and short invulnerability. A guard hit costs a heart and 100 points, with a recovery window.

Score consists of one-time objective awards, remaining-time bonus, first-use gadget variety, capped first-time device/robot tricks, and hit penalties. Repeating an already-scored action cannot farm points. Bronze marks a completed score, silver starts at 1,500 and gold at 1,900. First win unlocks pink; 4,000 combined best points unlock gold. Cosmetic settings alter the scarf only. Local scores are editable browser data, not authenticated rankings.

The first mission's active sign updates the HUD to meet the courier at Dock B. Pulsing markers, goal labels, gadget-range outlines, and short inline hints help without a tutorial wall. Sound is optional, synthesized only after a gesture, with mute in Options and Pause. Reduced-motion mode removes decorative movement; essential object movement remains.

Known scope: no co-op, editor, remote leaderboard, adaptive learned robot, background music, or claim of human-tested fun. Browser playtesting demonstrates usable controls and mission completion; a real novice playtest is the next quality gate.
