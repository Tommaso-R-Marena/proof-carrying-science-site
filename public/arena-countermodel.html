Build a world that breaks the translation.

  1. Read both statements. ∀ means every agent; ∃ means at least one; ¬ means NOT; → means IF…THEN.
  2. Choose the facts. Each agent is one row. Toggle P and Q, or a relation between two agents. A filled square is true.
  3. Ask the checker. You win when one statement is true and the other is false in the same world. Try fewer agents for a smaller witness.

    What was actually verified?

    The same deterministic finite first-order evaluator is run in the browser for practice and in the PCS Worker for consenting adult donations. It checks truth under an explicit Boolean interpretation of a finite domain with 1–3 agents, plus exhaustive smaller-domain search for minimality. It is not a Lean kernel run, not a proof of unbounded logical equivalence, and not evidence that an AI agent is safe in the real world. Full PCS formal claims require the separate Lean/PCS authority and CI boundary.

      OPTIONAL · HELP TRAIN FUTURE TRANSLATORS

      Donate a search trajectory, not a made-up proof.

      Your clicks, revisions, optional hints, and checker outcomes form one finite-model search trajectory. The PCS server replays the choices and recomputes every label. We never use a browser's submitted score as truth. Anyone can play locally without an account or upload.

      What exactly will be stored?

      After explicit opt-in from an email-verified account and adult self-attestation: mission and checker version, bounded move sequence, independently recomputed world states, hints and feedback-exposure flags, finite-model outcomes, and consent version. Guided walkthroughs and model suggestions are marked as hint assistance and excluded from unassisted model fitting. Identifiers are needed privately for deletion and abuse protection; owner-only training exports exclude account IDs and precise timestamps. This cannot certify that a human—not a script—made the moves, and consent may not retract already exported datasets.
