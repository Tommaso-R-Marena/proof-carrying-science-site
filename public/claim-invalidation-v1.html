Which claims must reopen when evidence changes?

PCS already computes conservative invalidation over declared dependencies. The open research problem is to make that semantics precise enough to review, adversarially test, and ultimately prove in Lean—without pretending the graph can reveal dependencies that were never represented.

REAL CORE OBLIGATION

Reachability should force reopening. Non-impact requires more.

If a changed artifact reaches workflow outputs, evidence, and a claim through declared dependency edges, that claim must be reconsidered. But the converse is stronger: saying an unreachable claim remains valid is sound only when the dependency graph is complete.

changed artifact
      ↓
declared workflow edges
      ↓
affected artifacts
      ↓
affected evidence
      ↓
claims that must reopen

unreachable ≠ unaffected
unless dependency completeness is explicit

What success means

Conservative invalidation becomes reviewable and formal.

  • Declared downstream impact is deterministic and adversarially tested.
  • The dependency-completeness premise is explicit rather than hidden.
  • Independent review attacks both false negatives and false positives.
  • The final Lean theorem states exactly what follows—and what does not.
What this does not claim

PCS cannot infer invisible dependencies.

  • A graph cannot prove it contains every dependency merely by being internally consistent.
  • Unreachable does not mean unaffected without a completeness premise.
  • Contributor consensus does not close an obligation.
  • A compiling theorem does not justify stronger prose than its exact statement.
