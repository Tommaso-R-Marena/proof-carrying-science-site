PCS / INTERVENTION PLANNER

What is the smallest
change that works?

Give each change a cost. Lock the facts you cannot change. Find a cheapest way to reach a logical goal, then discover which changes every optimal solution needs.

Your inputs and plans stay in this tab. Nothing is uploaded or saved. Variables and costs have the meanings you assign; a logical plan does not establish a real-world causal effect.

01 / SET THE CHALLENGE

Choose a mission, or design your own.

Either A or B reaches the goal. Changing A costs 3; changing B costs 1. Can you find the cheapest route?

  1. Write a goal with uppercase variables, AND, OR, NOT and ->. Add any required assumptions, one per line.
  2. Press “Build variable controls.” Set each starting value and positive change cost. Lock any value that must stay fixed.
  3. Try a proposal using the “Your plan” switches. Solve the minimum, then score your proposal against the exact optimum.

Up to 24 Boolean variables and eight assumptions. Costs are integers from 1 to 1,000,000. Every changed value pays its cost, including changes from true to false. A starting world may violate an assumption; your completed plan must satisfy them all.

“Mandatory” means changed in every optimal assignment. “Possible” means changed in at least one optimal assignment. These refer to the cheapest solutions, rather than all feasible worlds. When there is a tie, the displayed plan chooses false before true in sorted variable order. Variables that the diagram skips retain their starting values.

The exact bounded planner uses ordered decision diagrams and a single dynamic-programming pass. It may return an unresolved computation limit. A goal blocked by locks is infeasible; contradictory assumptions are reported separately. Neither earns a solution. A proposal earns an optimality gap only when it is feasible and the minimum is resolved.
