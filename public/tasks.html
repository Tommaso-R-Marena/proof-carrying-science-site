Task contract

Four things must be clear before productive work starts.

01

Scope

Exactly what artifact, analysis, test, proof, review, or annotation is expected.

02

Acceptance

How the result will be verified and what can cause rejection or escalation.

03

Compensation

Volunteer, funded bounty, paid review, or specialist contract—disclosed before work begins.

04

Rights + terms

Attribution, licensing/IP, confidentiality, payment timing, and review process stated up front.

Reliability, not punishment

Returning a task is allowed.

Tasks should not be held indefinitely, but volunteer accountability should not become coercion. A missed claim window returns the task to the pool. Repeated abandonment may reduce simultaneous reservations or create a cooldown. “Attempted but blocked” is valuable data and should not be treated like disappearing.

Gamification boundary

Leaderboards belong only where the score means something.

Ordinary tasks are not a points grind. PCS can use challenge-specific scoring only when a result is objectively checkable or independently validated—similar in spirit to scientific puzzle systems. Research quality, falsification, and difficult negative findings must never lose to raw task count.

CURRENT PILOT

Qualification can be demonstrated or reviewed—never self-declared.

L0–L1 open tasks can be started immediately and never become exclusive. For L2+ work, contributors may pass a variable auto-scored competency screening or submit the task application directly for manual review. Either way, final approval is manual, targets 1 business day and no later than 2 business days, and only then creates a reservation. Approved reserved work must show progress within 24 hours or it releases automatically.
