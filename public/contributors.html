Progression ladder

Start where you are. Advance by demonstrated reliability.

Every account begins at L0. The live backend—not a profile dropdown—stores the verified level. Ordinary promotion requires accepted work plus verified skills; exceptional fast-tracks require an explicit audited founder calibration override.

Promotion gates

Higher levels require evidence, not self-description.

These are the ordinary gates enforced by the backend. Founder overrides exist for genuinely qualified newcomers, but they are explicit and audit-logged rather than invisible exceptions.

L0

Automatic at account creation

Immediate access to open, non-exclusive entry work. Nothing at L0 can be monopolized by claiming it first.

L1

First accepted L0 contribution

Automatically earned after PCS accepts one real entry-level contribution.

L2

Two accepted contributions + one verified skill

This is the ordinary threshold for the general paid-task marketplace. A short synthetic calibration can verify a skill without pretending to be production work.

L3

Three accepted contributions, including one high-trust contribution

Signals that the contributor can handle work where mistakes have greater assurance impact.

L4

Verified review skill + two accepted high-trust contributions

Reviewer-authority eligibility. A level alone still does not authorize every review task.

L5

Verified specialist skill + three accepted high-trust contributions

Specialist status for areas such as Lean, security, ML evaluation, or computational biology. Exact task skills remain separately enforced.

L6

Explicit Founder/Owner appointment only

Research-lead technical authority always requires an audited Owner decision; it is never automatically accumulated. Only the Owner may later demote an L6.

L7 is not a contributor promotion.L7 is the display label for the single protected PCS Founder/Owner governance account. Technical progression ends at L6. Administrators and L6 research leads cannot create another Owner or modify the existing Owner.

What should count toward reputation?

Evidence quality, not activity theater.

  • A difficult reproduction that matches—or falsifies—the claim.
  • An adversarial case that exposes a real gap.
  • A Lean proof that compiles under the declared constraints.
  • A careful OPEN finding that prevents premature closure.
  • A reviewer decision later confirmed by independent review.
  • Helping another contributor produce better, more reproducible work.
Recognition

No public grind leaderboard.

A single high-quality falsification can matter more than one hundred easy confirmations.

DISCOVERY

First falsification

Recognize contributors who show that a claim, checker, decomposition, or assumption is wrong.

RIGOR

Independent reproduction

Recognize careful replication that confirms exact conditions—or exposes why reproduction fails.

COMMUNITY

Mentorship + review

Recognize people who improve others' work without becoming a popularity contest.

COMMON GOOD

The goal is to grow people into harder safety work, not extract free labor.

Some contributors will remain volunteers. Others should be able to move into bounties, paid review, specialist contracts, stipends, or employment as their contribution depth grows and funding allows.
