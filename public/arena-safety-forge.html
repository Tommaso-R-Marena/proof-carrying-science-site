RESEARCH / ADULT OPT-IN

Your experiments could inform future AI-safety research.

This game captures the *structure* of a useful learning signal: a decision state, a proposed action or policy edit, and independently replayed feedback. Unlike a fixed quiz, the space includes failures, partial attempts, multiple valid repairs, and entirely new scenario seeds.

What data is shared? How can I delete it?

Practice is local and private by default. There is no auto-upload of actions, game state, or puzzle outcomes. Donation requires a clearly labeled adult (18+) research switch, a signed-in PCS account with verified email, and a separate submit click. No minors' gameplay is collected for research.

With your consent, PCS stores one bounded session's seed, attempted action sequences and policy configurations. The server independently replays each choice and computes the labels; it does not trust your device's score. We also label whether hints or earlier checker feedback were revealed before your choice, so assisted and unassisted examples are not confused. Data remains account-linked for erasure, but Owner-only research exports remove account identifiers and precise times. This dataset can support experiments in synthetic counterexample search and guard selection, not teach a model real Lean tactics or certify AI alignment.

What would make this useful to a real model?

01 · Explore, don't memorize

Different seeds change worlds, risk budgets, and existing guards. The same visible action can be safe or unsafe in a different state.

02 · Discover counterexamples

Both successes and unsuccessful search traces teach which decisions led to a safety violation, as checked by a deterministic simulator.

03 · Verify repairs independently

An exhaustive finite-state evaluator finds missed cases and confirms that the legitimate goal remains achievable. No reward for simply disabling everything.

04 · Keep science honest

Game traces are synthetic supervision only. Real progress requires held-out evaluations, explicit trust assumptions, and eventually Lean-kernel checks on actual proof objects.
