A clearer way to check important claims

PCS helps you show why a result should be trusted—not just say “trust me.”

In plain English: you make a specific claim. PCS keeps that claim tied to the exact files and checks behind it, reruns the checks a reviewer can rerun, and reports what passed, what failed, and what is still unknown.

PCS IN ONE MINUTE

Think of it like a tamper-evident lab notebook for important computational claims.

Suppose someone says an AI system obeyed a safety rule, or a scientific model produced a result. A screenshot or a green checkmark is easy to misunderstand. PCS asks: What exact claim are you making? What exact evidence supports it? Can another person rerun the relevant checks? What assumptions are still being trusted?

The key idea: PCS does not decide reality for you. It makes a bounded claim and its evidence harder to separate, easier to rerun, and harder to overstate.

Trust without hand-waving

What is proved, what is checked, and what is still trusted are different things.

The strongest current theorem starts from raw canonical archive bytes and reaches scoped high assurance with a single explicit hypothesis: NoForgery for the Lean Ed25519 trust anchor. SHA-256/FIPS equivalence, canonical STORED ZIP decoding, and replay semantics for all six built-ins are proved in Lean; the remaining boundaries stay explicit.

Open Trust Center See architecture
PROVED / LEANMachine-checked
  • canonical v0.6/v2 representation + exact archive partition
  • SHA-256 = independent FIPS 180-4 specification
  • canonical STORED ZIP decoding from raw bytes
  • all six built-in replay semantics in Lean
  • PK/PD match → real analytic model within tolerance
  • normalized decisions → scoped high assurance
EXPLICIT TCBNamed boundaries
  • Ed25519 unforgeability (NoForgery)
  • Lean Ed25519 ↔ independent RFC 8032 specification
  • workflow front-end analysis producing normalized FreshSource
  • environment meaning beyond verified EnvFacts
  • external/non-built-in validators and legacy non-canonical ZIPs
NOT CLAIMEDScientific adequacy
  • biological or clinical truth
  • regulatory approval or GxP validation
  • correctness of arbitrary scientific code

AI safety first · domain-general by design

Start with one bounded claim that matters—and let more people help verify it.

PCS is not trying to “prove arbitrary AI safety” or arbitrary science. The immediate focus is bounded, high-consequence AI-safety claims where exact replay, formal semantics, adversarial testing, provenance, and independent review can create real assurance. The same kernel is intended to support additional computational disciplines through explicit domain adapters.
