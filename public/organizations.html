Access model

People should not need money to participate in public-interest assurance.

PCS is being designed so that students, individuals, academic researchers, nonprofits, and public-interest projects can participate broadly without a paywall, while large commercial organizations pay for enterprise value and help fund the shared infrastructure.

PUBLIC / COMMUNITY

Broadly free

For individuals, students, educators, public-interest researchers, nonprofits, and open community work.

  • contributor onboarding and training
  • public task marketplace
  • public assurance cases
  • browser verification and educational tools
  • public research and trust documentation
Goal: no paywall for participation
SMALL / RESEARCH TEAMS

Low-friction access

Early-stage and research teams should be able to experiment without enterprise procurement becoming the barrier.

  • public tooling and workflows
  • bounded support where capacity allows
  • pilot pathways
  • clear upgrade boundary when private scale/support is needed
Exact eligibility policy to be published
LARGE COMMERCIAL

Paid enterprise use

Organizations that need private deployment, scale, organizational controls, dedicated support, or compliance-oriented features should pay.

  • private assurance cases
  • SSO / RBAC / organizational governance
  • private deployments and support
  • audit / reporting integrations
  • higher-scale verification and SLAs
Revenue helps sustain the commons
What enterprises pay for

Charge for organizational value, not for access to truth.

The public-good model works best when enterprise pricing attaches to costly, organization-specific services rather than withholding basic safety knowledge from everyone else.

PRIVATE

Private infrastructure

Dedicated environments, confidential evidence handling, deployment support, and organization-specific controls.

SCALE

Higher-volume assurance

Operational capacity, CI integration, higher throughput, dedicated support, and reliability commitments.

GOVERNANCE

Enterprise controls

Role-based review, audit exports, identity integration, policy management, and compliance-oriented workflows.

What paying never buys

A predetermined result.

  • No sponsor override of failed checks.
  • No paid shortcut around formal/replay requirements.
  • No right to suppress an OPEN or falsified result in a public assurance case.
  • No special authority merely because the organization funds PCS.
Cross-subsidy target

Make commercial success improve public capacity.

The operating goal is to dedicate a transparent share of commercial surplus to public infrastructure, contributor bounties, independent review, training, and open safety benchmarks. The exact percentage should be set only after the cost base, taxes, legal structure, and sustainability requirements are known.

See Safety Bounty Fund
Licensing caution

The mission is clear; the legal mechanism is not frozen yet.

Current policy boundary:PCS should not describe a commercial-use restriction as “open source” if the final license does not meet open-source definitions. The project may use an open-source core plus paid services, dual licensing, source-available terms, or another structure after counsel review. This page describes the desired access economics, not final license language.
