Common Good Governance

The crowd can supply work. It cannot vote a claim into truth.

The social governance of PCS should mirror its technical trust model: provenance, explicit authority, conflict disclosure, independent checking, and visible uncertainty.

PCS
COMMON GOOD COMMITMENT · DRAFT PUBLIC POLICY

Participation should be broadly accessible. Assurance should remain rigorous. Labor should be treated fairly.

Proof-Carrying Science exists to improve the reliability and safety of consequential computational systems. Individuals should not be excluded from contributing to or benefiting from public-interest work because they cannot pay. Public research, education, contributor training, and core AI-safety work should remain broadly accessible. Large commercial users should help sustain the infrastructure and paid safety work from which the wider ecosystem benefits.

This commitment is a governance policy, not a substitute for final software licensing, tax, employment, or contractual terms. Those require separate legal review.

Non-negotiable principle

Crowdsource the work. Never the authority.

No human, AI model, funder, employer, contributor level, or majority vote can make a PCS obligation true by declaration.

Authority hierarchy

Technical competence and organizational control are deliberately separate.

No contributor rank can become Founder/Owner authority by accumulation, vote, payment, or self-declaration.

L0–L6

Technical and research authority

Contributor levels track demonstrated competence and scoped responsibility. L6 is Research Lead, but an L6 does not automatically receive website administration or ownership power.

L7

Unique Founder / Owner governance authority

L7 is a display label for the single protected Owner account, not a technical rank. The Owner alone can appoint or demote L6 research leads, grant or revoke administrator authority, suspend or restore accounts, and modify other administrators.

ADMIN

Delegated operational authority

Administrators can perform the bounded review and coordination functions delegated to them, but they cannot alter the Owner, create another Owner, or modify L6/peer-admin authority unless the Owner acts.

TRUTH

Governance never determines verification truth

The Owner can govern people, permissions, assignments, and infrastructure. The Owner cannot turn a failed or OPEN obligation into a verified result by decree; PCS proof/evidence semantics remain independent.

Compensation policy

Volunteerism should be a choice, not the hidden price of belonging.

1

Disclose compensation before work begins.

Every productive task is labeled volunteer, funded bounty, paid review, specialist contract, or explicitly planned/unfunded.

2

Do not require indefinite unpaid productive work for advancement.

Small training and qualification exercises are reasonable. Large unpaid gates are not.

3

Open the general paid marketplace at L2.

L2 means a contributor has demonstrated enough reliability for bounded compensated tasks. It does not grant review authority.

4

Reserve independent review authority for L4+.

Review authority is a trust role and should require demonstrated judgment, reproducibility, conflict management, and reliability.

5

Protect negative findings.

Payment or sponsor expectations cannot depend on turning an assurance case green. A valid falsification or OPEN result can fully satisfy a task.

Reliability system

Accountability without coercion.

The default is one active task at a time, roughly one bounded contribution per week for new contributors. If a claimed task expires, it returns to the pool. Repeated abandonment can reduce simultaneous reservations or create a cooldown. “Attempted but blocked” should be recorded separately because it may reveal documentation or task-design failures.

No monetary penalty for ordinary volunteer non-completion.Reliability protects scarce tasks without turning a volunteer commons into debt or coercion.
Conflict rules

Separate contribution, review, and funding where risk warrants it.

High-consequence assurance cases should disclose who funded the work, who produced the evidence, who reviewed it, and whether a reviewer has a conflict. The same person may contribute and review in low-risk contexts, but the role should be explicit rather than invisible.

Restricted surfaces

Some things should not be crowdsourced openly.

KEYS + AUTHORITY

Production signing and trust-kernel control

Private keys, authority configuration, and trust-kernel merge rights remain tightly controlled.

SENSITIVE DATA

Confidential partner and high-risk material

Least-privilege access, explicit agreements, and appropriate handling controls apply.

VULNERABILITIES

Security-sensitive findings

Responsible disclosure comes before public task-board visibility where premature release could cause harm.

TRUST RULE

Money, seniority, and popularity never substitute for evidence.

The technical verifier and the social governance should reinforce the same principle: authority must be explicit and conclusions must remain scoped to what was actually checked.
